    How the ratio is calculated

    The predicted point-to-cost ratio is the selected drivers’ predicted Las Vegas GP points divided by their total price.

    Ratio = (points₁ + points₂ + points₃) / (price₁ + price₂ + price₃)

    Points are each driver’s projected Las Vegas GP points from the case file vegas_website_driver_data.csv. Prices are the case price_millions figures. A price of 60 is $60 million. Nothing is rescaled.

    Several point totals are fractional — 12.6667, 0.1667, 4.3333. Those are expected points, an average across race scenarios, not a single finishing place. This page does not invent a second points model. It applies the case formula to those supplied forecasts.

    On the slip, a driver’s contribution is their projected points divided by the trio’s total price. The three contributions sum to the ratio. A driver’s own points-per-million is shown as well, but own ratios are not added together. That would be a different number, and it is not the case formula.

    The yield gap is share of the trio’s points minus share of the trio’s price. Positive means that driver is pulling this trio’s ratio up relative to the other two. It can disagree with a high own ratio: a good-value driver can still dilute an even cheaper trio.

    Rank compares the finished trio with all possible combinations of three drivers, using the same division. Displayed figures are rounded. The ratio itself uses full precision.
